## 1. INTRODUCTION

This document presents the semester project developed under a **Project-Based Learning (PBL)** methodology.

The project brings together contributions from four supporting curricular units—**Generative AI**, **Applied Deep Learning**, **Interface Design and Interaction**, and **Emerging Technologies**—coordinated through **Project II** as the integrating and management framework.

Together, these courses support the conception, design, and development of an **interactive Emergent Technology experience**.

The overarching goal is for students to produce a **fully functional interactive prototype** that demonstrates both **technical competence** and **creative intention**.

Students are expected to combine methods and practices from Generative AI, Applied Deep Learning, Emerging Technologies, Interface Design and Interaction, and project planning and production within Project II, in order to develop an experience that is **coherent**, **meaningful**, and **engaging** for a public audience.

### Each course contributes essential knowledge and skills:

- **Generative AI**  
  Introduces prompt-based workflows and creative uses of generative systems to support ideation, concept development, and asset production (e.g., text, images, sound, or video), as well as critical reflection on authorship, ethics, and limitations.

- **Applied Deep Learning**  
  Supports the understanding and practical application of generative models (e.g., CNN-based approaches, GANs, VAEs, diffusion models) for creating, transforming, or driving content and behaviours within the experience.

- **Emerging Technologies**  
  Explores VR/AR ecosystems and sensor-based interaction, including body/hand tracking, spatial input, and relevant hardware/software frameworks, enabling students to integrate modalities such as hand tracking, eye tracking, or haptic feedback where appropriate.

- **Interface Design and Interaction**  
  Focuses on user experience and interaction quality, ensuring that interactions are intuitive, comfortable, and accessible in immersive contexts. This includes affordances, feedback, usability testing, and iterative refinement to support immersion and legibility.

- **Project II**  
  Provides the integration and project management layer, guiding students through planning, prototyping, production, documentation, and iteration. It ensures that the contributions from the other courses converge into a cohesive final artefact.

### By the end of the semester, students are expected to have:

- Developed a working **Emergent Technology product** that meaningfully integrates interaction design, emerging technologies, and generative AI/deep learning components.
- Gained hands-on experience with **Unity** and immersive development workflows.
- Conducted **usability testing** and iterative improvements informed by observation and feedback.
- Strengthened collaboration, communication, and project management skills through interdisciplinary teamwork.
- Prepared the prototype for a **public exhibition**, including presentation materials and a clearly communicated concept.

This document outlines the project theme, technical expectations, milestones, and evaluation criteria to support a structured development process and ensure readiness for the end-of-semester public showcase.

## 2. THEME

The results of these projects will be integrated into the fashion event calendar organized by the **Master’s in Branding and Fashion Design**, who will be responsible for the event’s production and curation.

Students from the **MCCIA** are expected to develop an **exploratory and speculative project**, suitable for public presentation as an **interactive exhibition experience**.

The theme and output format are open, but **must relate to the fashion universe** in some way. Examples of potential topics to address include:

- **Identity** — In what ways does our expression of identity evolve with technology?
- **Body** — How does technology become a bodily extension, changing the way the body is perceived and presented?
- **Society** — How will technology impact how we build community, relate to one another, and communicate through what we wear?
- **Culture** — How will new technologies impact the communication of cultural concepts and messages?
- **Wearables** — In what ways will technology be used functionally and/or communicatively on the body?
- **Industry 5.0** — How will humans and intelligent machines collaborate in creative and productive processes?
- **Consumption** — How will technology alter the shopping experience for fashion products?

In the first phase, students must decide the topic they would like to speculate about, find information that supports it (critically and theoretically) and define what kind of technologies they would like to work with.

## 3. Project Phases

### Phase 1: Research and Concept Development (Weeks 1–3)

Students should:

- Research possible methods of **Emergent Technologies**, selecting one that will serve as a reference for the development of the experience — each group should try to select a **different** technology.
- Present references for style, image, color, sound, etc. for the experience.
- Present the **user flow** of the experience (what the user does, how they interact, what are the outcomes, etc.).

### Phase 2: Prototyping and Technical Exploration (Weeks 3–7)

Students should:

- Create a list of tasks required to complete a functional project by the end of the semester.
- Identify possible problems and project necessities.
- Develop a **functional prototype** that demonstrates the core functionalities of the product.

### Phase 3: Refinement and User Experience (Weeks 8–11)

Students should:

- Refine the project to a **final product state**.
- Test the project with other users to identify usability problems (issues from not knowing how to use it).

### Phase 4: Presentation and Exhibition (Weeks 12–14)

Students should:

- Prepare the project for the final presentation.
- Identify any special needs required for the presentation and fulfill them.

## 4. PROJECT REQUIREMENTS

Within the context of the collaboration between the two master programs, groups may propose solutions and strategies for any undefined aspects, but **must comply** with the following:

- Team size: **2 students** (exceptions only if strictly needed).
- The prototype **must be testable**.
- **Graphical and audio assets** must be integrated.
- All assets must be **original** or **legally obtained** from third parties.

Strategic decisions should be presented to the project professors as soon as possible for acceptance and initial feedback.
