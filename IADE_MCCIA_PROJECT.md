### 1. INTRODUCTION

This document presents the semester project developed under a Project-Based Learning (PBL) methodology. The project brings together contributions from four supporting curriculum units: "Generative AI, Applied Deep Learning, Emerging Technologies, Interface Design, and Interaction..."

The overarching goal is for a student to produce a fully functional interactive prototype that demonstrates both technical competence and creative intent. Students are expected to incorporate methods and practices from Generative AI and deep learning, Emerging Technologies, Interface Design, Interaction, and project planning within Project II, in order to develop an experience that is coherent, meaningful, and engaging for a public audience.

#### By the end of the semester, students are expected to have:

*   *Developed a working Emergent Technology product that meaningfully integrates interaction design, concept development, and asset production (e.g., text images, sound, or video), as well as visual reflection on authority, ethics, and limitations.*
*   *Appreciated how the project supports understanding and practical application of generative models (e.g., CNN-based approaches, GANs, VAEs), differentiation layers for creating, transforming, or driving content and behaviours within the experience.*
*   *Emergent Technologies explores VR/AR ecosystems and sensor hardware, including body/hand tracking, spatial input, and relevant software/frameworks, enabling students to integrate modalities such as hand tracking, eye tracking, or Haptic feedback where appropriate.*

#### Interface Design and Interaction focus on user experience and interaction quality, ensuring that interactions are intuitive, comfortable, and accessible in immersive contexts. This includes affordances, feedback, usability testing, and iterative refinement to support immersion and legibility.

#### Project II provides the integration and project management layer, guiding students through planning, prototyping, production, documentation, and iteration. It ensures that the contributions from the other courses converge into a cohesive final artifact.
